What 12 months of ride data tells us, and how to act on it

For the Marketing and Executive Teams · Nov 2025 – Oct 2026
The headline

Casual riders ride longer. Members ride steadier.

Business question: how do the two groups use Cyclistic differently, and how do we convert casual riders?

Finding 1

Casual rides are longer and follow the seasons

Average ride length by month (minutes). Casual peaks May–Jul; members stay flat.

Finding 2

Casual riders are weekend-leisure riders

Number of months (out of 12) each day was the busiest day of the week

Casual riders: Sunday in 9 months, Wednesday their quietest in 10. Members split between Saturday and Sunday.

What it means

Two different riders, two different needs

Casual riders look like leisure users; members look like routine riders.

Casual rider

  • Long, relaxed rides
  • Weekends and summer
  • Pays per ride or day
  • Rides less often than members

Annual member

  • Shorter, consistent rides
  • Rides more often, steady all year
  • Predictable revenue for Cyclistic
  • Bike is part of a routine
Recommendations

Three ways to convert casual riders

Each action is tied to a finding.

Finding
Action
Casual use peaks on Sundays and in summer
1. Pilot a summer weekend membership that matches how they already ride
Casual rides run about 60% longer
2. Show the savings: long rides cost more per trip than a membership
Long rides are where the value is clearest
3. Prompt in the app right after a long ride with a membership offer
Casual use drops in Dec and Feb
Support: off-season perks so winter isn’t a reason to say no
Next steps

Start small, measure, then scale

Proposed pilot

  • 3-month summer weekend pass test
  • Success metric: % of casual riders who convert within 90 days
  • A/B test “save money” vs. “ride more often” messaging
  • Review results in the fall

Limits and data to add

  • Most monthly maximums sit just under 60 minutes, suggesting capped data
  • Add rider surveys
